## What moved and why

Friday's selloff rotated from tech to the rest of the market: the Nasdaq steadied, down just 0.21% after Thursday's 2.60% AI-capex rout, while the Dow fell 652 points (−1.23%) as surging crude — WTI up 3.77% to $103.22 and Brent to $116.72 on Houthi attacks on Saudi energy sites and renewed U.S.–Iran strike fears — stoked inflation worries ahead of next week's Fed meeting. Energy was the only real winner (S&P energy sector +2.60% to a record), while consumer staples (−1.92%), discretionary and financials each fell well over 1%, and the 10-year yield climbed back to ~4.99%.

## Week in review

A split week: the S&P 500 fell ~0.5% and the Nasdaq ~0.7% for the week, while the Dow rose ~0.4% for its third straight weekly gain. Monday–Wednesday gains were erased Thursday, when the S&P fell 1.21% and the Nasdaq 2.60% — its worst day since June — on doubts about financing and returns on debt-funded AI spending, led by Meta's ~7% drop. Next up: the FOMC decision on October 28, plus earnings from Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon.
